Pfeffer Instructions

History

Pfeffer, also known as Pepper or Hasenpfeffer, is a traditional North American card game and a regional variation of Bid Euchre. It is especially popular in the American Midwest, including the Great Lakes region and the states of Minnesota, Iowa, Wisconsin, and Michigan.

Euchre itself traces back to Jucker, an Alsatian card game brought to the United States by German immigrants in the 19th century.

The German word hasenpfeffer refers to a marinated rabbit or hare stew. The card game's name may also be associated with the German expression “Hase im Pfeffer,” meaning “in a pickle.”

Overview

Pfeffer is a trick-taking game for four players arranged in two partnerships. It uses a 24-card deck, and the first team to reach 31 points wins.

Players

The game is played by four players in two teams, with the partners seated opposite each other.

Note: In this game, West is Dave, North is Ivy, East is John, and South is You. Dave and John are partners; Ivy and you are partners.

Cards

The deck contains 24 cards: A, K, Q, J, 10, and 9 in each of the four suits.

The jack of trump, called the right bower, is the highest trump. The jack of the other suit of the same color, called the left bower, is the second-highest trump and counts as a card of the trump suit for the hand. If spades are trump, the ranking from highest to lowest is J♠, J♣, A♠, K♠, Q♠, 10♠, 9♠.

In the non-trump suits, the ranking is: A (high), K, Q, J (if it is not the left bower), 10, 9.

Dealing

The first dealer is chosen at random. The dealer distributes the cards one at a time, beginning with the player on the dealer's left and proceeding clockwise, until each player has six cards. After each hand is played and scored, the deal passes to the player on the left.

Bidding

A bid states the number of tricks the player's team contracts to win and gives the winning bidder the right to choose a trump suit or no trump. Starting with the player to the dealer's left, each player may bid or pass. In a trump suit, the minimum bid is three; in no trump, the minimum bid is four. Regular bids range from three through five in trump and from four through five in no trump. Pfeffer is the bid to take all six tricks as a team.

Each player in turn must either bid higher than the current high bid or pass. There is one round of bidding. The highest bidder then names a trump suit or chooses no trump.

Note: Bids are compared as plain numbers, regardless of trump or no trump — a new bid must be numerically higher than the current high bid to overcall it. Pfeffer always outranks any bid of five.

If all four players pass, the cards are thrown in and there is a new deal by the next dealer in turn.

Play

After bidding, the highest bidder names a trump suit or declares no trump, then makes the opening lead and may play any card. Play proceeds clockwise. Each other player must follow the suit led if possible; a player who cannot follow suit may play any card. The highest trump wins the trick. If no trump is played, the highest card of the suit led wins. The winner of each trick leads the next trick.

For purposes of following suit, the left bower belongs to the trump suit, not to the suit printed on the card.

Example: If spades are trump, the jack of spades is the right bower and the highest trump. The jack of clubs is the left bower and the second-highest trump; for this hand, it counts as a spade rather than a club. The remaining trumps rank A♠, K♠, Q♠, 10♠, 9♠. Because the jack of clubs is treated as a spade, it cannot be used to follow a club lead. When a spade is led, a player holding the jack of clubs has a spade and may use it to follow suit; if it is that player's only spade, the player must use it. If the jack of clubs is led, the other players must follow with spades when able, and players with no spades may play any card.

Scoring

Both teams start the game at 0 points. If the bidding team meets or exceeds its contract, it scores one point for each trick it takes. If it fails to make its contract, it subtracts the amount of its bid from its score; scores may therefore be negative. Regardless of whether the contract succeeds, the opposing team scores one point for each trick it takes.

Note: A failed Pfeffer bid subtracts 6 points, since Pfeffer is the bid to take all six tricks.

The first team to score 31 or more points wins. If both sides reach 31 or more points on the same hand, the side with the higher score wins. If their scores are equal, the game is a tie.
