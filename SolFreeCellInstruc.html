FreeCell Instructions

History

FreeCell was invented by Paul Alfille in 1978 while he was a medical student at the University of Illinois. Alfille built it for the Control Data PLATO educational computer system, making FreeCell one of the earliest computer card games ever designed.

The game became widely popular when Jim Horne included it in the Microsoft Windows Entertainment Pack in 1991, then made it a standard game in Windows 95.

Overview

The objective is to move all 52 cards to four Foundation piles, one for each suit, building each pile from Ace to King. To accomplish this, reveal and arrange cards in the Tableau and use the Free Cells for temporary storage. The game ends when all cards are on the Foundations or no legal moves remain.

Players

FreeCell is a single-player game.

Cards

FreeCell uses a standard 52-card deck. Cards can be moved by dragging and dropping them.

Autoplay is a feature that automatically moves eligible cards to the foundations. The Autoplay button toggles it ON or OFF. The default is ON.

Setup

Deal all 52 cards face-up into eight Tableau columns. The first four columns get seven cards each; the remaining four columns get six each. All cards are visible throughout.

The Tableau

Build the eight Tableau columns in descending rank while alternating colors. You may move an exposed card onto an exposed card of the next higher rank and opposite color. You may also move a stack when every card in it already follows that descending, alternating-color pattern.

Example: The 10 of hearts may be played on the Jack of clubs or the Jack of spades. The 5 of clubs may be played on the 6 of hearts or the 6 of diamonds.

The maximum number of cards you can move as a stack equals the number of empty Free Cells plus one.

When a Tableau column is empty, any single card or valid sequential stack can be moved to the empty space.

Free Cells

The four Free Cells are above and to the left of the Tableau. You may temporarily place any single exposed card in an empty Free Cell. A card in a Free Cell remains available and may be moved to the Tableau or a Foundation whenever the destination move is legal. Each Free Cell holds only one card.

Foundations

The four Foundation piles are above and to the right of the Tableau. Each Foundation contains one suit and is built in ascending order from Ace through King. When an Ace becomes available, move it to an empty Foundation, then add the 2 through 10, Jack, Queen, and King of the same suit in order.

You may move a card from a Foundation back to the Tableau if the move follows the Tableau rule: descending rank and alternating color. You may also move eligible Tableau cards to the Foundations.

Note: If Autoplay is ON and you need to move one or more cards from a Foundation, turn Autoplay OFF first. Move the card or cards to the Tableau, make the intended Tableau play, and then turn Autoplay back ON.

Game Over

The game ends in one of two ways. You win when all 52 cards are in the four Foundation piles. You lose when no legal moves remain in the Tableau or between the Tableau and Foundations.
