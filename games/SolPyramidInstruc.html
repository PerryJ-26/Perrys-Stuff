Pyramid Instructions

History

Pyramid’s exact origins are unclear, but the game has appeared in card game collections since at least the early 1900s. It was first formally documented in print in 1949 in The Complete Book of Solitaire and Patience Games. Its association with Egyptian imagery likely comes from the game’s triangular layout rather than any actual Egyptian heritage.

The game achieved broad software recognition when a variation called Tut’s Tomb was included in the Microsoft Entertainment Pack 2 in 1991.

Overview

The objective is to remove every card from the Pyramid, the Stock pile, and the Waste pile. Kings are removed by themselves; every other card is removed in a pair with another card whose values together total 13. The game ends when those three piles are empty, or when no pair, King, or redeal remains available.

Players

Pyramid is a single-player game.

Cards

The game uses one standard 52-card deck. For pairing purposes, suit never matters - only rank.

Card Values:

  • Ace: 1
  • Number Cards (2–10): Face value
  • Jack: 11
  • Queen: 12
  • King: 13 (a King is removed by itself, not paired with another card)

Setup

Deal 28 cards face up in seven overlapping rows to form the Pyramid: one card in the top row, two in the next row, and one more card in each following row until the seventh (bottom) row holds seven cards. Place the remaining 24 cards face down in the Stock pile at the upper left.

A card is exposed once no other card covers any part of it. The seven cards in the bottom row are exposed from the very start. Every other card becomes exposed only after both of the cards resting on it - the two cards directly beneath it in the row below - have themselves been removed.

Pairing Rules

Select two available cards whose values total 13, regardless of suit. The selected pair moves to the Discard pile, face up, at the upper right.

Valid Pairs:

  • Ace and Queen
  • 2 and Jack
  • 3 and 10
  • 4 and 9
  • 5 and 8
  • 6 and 7

Cards available for pairing are:

  • two exposed (not covered by any other card) Pyramid cards;
  • the top card of the Stock pile and an exposed Pyramid card;
  • the top card of the Waste pile and an exposed Pyramid card;
  • the top card of the Stock pile and the top card of the Waste pile.

A King is removed by itself rather than paired. A King may be removed from the Pyramid or from the top of the Waste pile. A King in the Stock pile must first be turned onto the Waste pile before it can be removed. Like a pair, a removed King moves to the Discard pile face up.

Gameplay

Click the Stock pile to turn its top card face up onto the Waste pile below it. The top card of the Waste pile is then available for pairing until it, too, is removed or covered by the next card turned from the Stock.

When the Stock pile is empty, click it again to return the Waste cards to the Stock pile without shuffling. You may do this twice more, for a total of three passes through the Stock.

Game Over

Win: There are no more cards in the Pyramid, the Stock pile, or the Waste pile.

Lose: Cards remain in the Pyramid, the Stock pile, or the Waste pile, and no valid pair, King, or redeal is available.
